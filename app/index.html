Settlement trough

Screening, stage 1

Three-stage assessment after Mair et al. (1996): preliminary assessment (screening), second stage assessment, detailed evaluation. Limits of the first stage after Rankin (1988), both as given by Franzius (2003), §2.4.2: maximum slope and maximum settlement at the building ⇒ negligible risk of damage; otherwise a second stage assessment is required.

Assumptions

  • Greenfield movements, no interaction between ground and building; the building follows the trough.
  • Two tunnels: the troughs are superposed independently; interaction between closely spaced tunnels (narrow pillar) is not considered.
  • Tilt = slope of the chord over the whole building (beam assumption, Cording et al. 2010, Fig. 5b). If the distortion concentrates in one bay (Fig. 5a), the tilt is smaller and β is underestimated; Cording et al. report β between 2Δ/L and 4Δ/L.
  • Only extension counts: compressive lateral strain is set to zero.
  • fL reduces the lateral strain; fL = 0 for floors tied to the walls or a grade beam (Cording et al. 2010, Fig. 2).
  • Screening (stage 1 of the three-stage assessment after Mair et al. 1996) with the limits after Rankin (1988), see above.
  • Not included: subsurface trough at foundation level, longitudinal trough, deflection ratio method.
